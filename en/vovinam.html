Vovinam

Introductory Article:

Source: CNN

• Introduction:

Vovinam is a martial art that embodies the spirit and pride of the Vietnamese people, with a profound influence across the world. With a long and storied history, Vovinam has become an indispensable part of Vietnam’s cultural and athletic identity. As a locality that has made significant contributions to the preservation, development, and international promotion of this martial art, the Council of Masters and the community of practitioners in Ho Chi Minh City have been honored and recognized for their outstanding dedication.

Source: danviet.vn

• History and Development:

Việt Võ Đạo – Vovinam is a major martial arts school in Vietnam, founded by Grandmaster Nguyễn Lộc in 1938 in Hanoi. Later, it became known simply as Vovinam, which consists of two parts: Vietnamese Martial Arts (Việt Võ Thuật) and Vietnamese Martial Spirit (Việt Võ Đạo). However, the growth and flourishing of this martial art are closely tied to the land of Saigon (now Ho Chi Minh City). In 1960, the late Grandmaster Lê Sáng succeeded as head of the school, continuing the mission of promoting and expanding Vovinam. Together with other masters, he worked to research and systematize the theory, techniques, and philosophy of the art, laying a solid foundation for Vietnamese martial traditions. The Vovinam Masters Council was established in 2010, marking a new milestone in the history of Vietnamese martial arts. The late Chief Master Nguyễn Văn Chiếu, along with other masters, spread Vovinam widely to international friends across five continents. With its national identity, scientific foundation, practicality, and creativity, along with a philosophy of benevolence, honor, and self-sacrifice, Vovinam – Việt Võ Đạo has contributed to introducing the country and people of Vietnam to the international community.
The movement to learn Vovinam has expanded and developed widely across the world, truly becoming a cultural bridge fostering mutual understanding among nations. To date, Vovinam is present in more than 70 countries and territories worldwide, with over 2.5 million practitioners training and continuing to grow.
The establishment of the World Vovinam Federation in 2008 marked a turning point for the global development of the Vovinam movement, followed by the formation of continental federations in Asia, Europe, and Africa. Since then, international competition systems such as continental and world championships have been regularly held, with increasing quantity and quality. Furthermore, Vovinam has also become a traditional sport in the SEA Games arena.

• Technical Features of Vovinam

Based on the foundation of Vietnamese traditional martial arts and wrestling, while simultaneously researching the essence of other martial arts from around the world to absorb, adapt, and neutralize—especially improving its own technical foundation according to the principle of the Harmony between Hard and Soft (Cương – Nhu phối triển)—the technical system (techniques, forms...) of the Vovinam school is quite rich, diverse, and bears distinct characteristics.

• Practicality of Vovinam

This is the most distinctive feature of Vovinam. Instead of spending a long period practicing stances and forms before learning self-defense scenarios, Vovinam students are instructed by their trainers right away in countering and escape techniques (when being grabbed by the hair, collar, hand, choked, held around the waist...), as well as basic counterattacks (when punched, kicked, stomped...) alongside fundamental techniques like blocking, punching, kicking, chopping, falling... right from the very first training sessions. This was a remarkably innovative mindset of the late master Nguyễn Lộc in the late 1930s, aiming to help practitioners effectively defend themselves immediately. This practicality not only suited the social conditions of that era but remains even more reasonable and valuable in the modern age, as students do not just focus time on martial arts training but also have many other essential needs and duties such as: learning other fields (culture, profession...) as well as entertainment and working to make a living...

• Continuity (Linked Sequences)

The next distinctive feature is continuity. A Vovinam technique launched must always consist of at least 3 actions.
For example: to counter an opponent’s straight right punch, a student will step the left foot to the left while simultaneously using the right hand to deflect the opponent's punching hand to avoid it; then counterattack by using the left hand to chop at the eye or face and finish with a low right-hand punch to the opponent’s abdomen; or Strategy No. 1 (continuous attack) includes a downward hand chop to the eye or face, adding a low right-hand punch to the belly and stepping the right foot forward to use the right elbow to strike the opponent’s temple.
In general, these can be continuous movements using hands (chop, jab, punch, snap, elbow...), or feet (kick, stomp, sweep, hook...), or hand techniques accompanied by foot techniques (chop + sweep, takedown...). This way of striking aims to secure superiority in self-defense and combat, suits the compact and quick agility of Vietnamese people, and is also a measure to provide a backup in case the first 1 or 2 initial strikes miss the target.

• The Principle of Harmony Between Hard and Soft in Vovinam

The Vovinam technical system also adheres to the principle of Harmony between Hard and Soft (Cương – Nhu phối triển). When attacked, a student often evades (Soft), then counterattacks (Hard). Additionally, Vovinam has many attack techniques that still ensure this principle; for instance, when launching an attack kick or counter-kick (Hard) to the opponent's body, the student must use their hands to cover their face and protect their groin to defend (Soft). Even in falling techniques (safe, painless), the student must tense up and curl the body (Hard), then roll the body upon impact (Soft) to dissipate the opponent's attack force and the falling weight. Thanks to this, Vovinam students practice techniques and falls on normal tile floors just as they would on mats.
In other words, the Vovinam technical system includes soft, flexible moves and hard, powerful strikes, and even within each individual technique, there is a combination of Hard and Soft, similar to the interaction of Yin and Yang in nature and society. The Harmony of Hard and Soft does not simply imply containing both hard and soft qualities, but rather being flexible and transformative. Sometimes more Hard, less Soft; sometimes less Hard, more Soft; sometimes half Hard, half Soft, depending on the specific situation. This principle is also reflected in the spiritual life and conduct of Vovinam students because: "Hard symbolizes heroism, steely will, determination, and the Courage of a martial artist. Soft symbolizes gentleness, calmness, and the Benevolence of a warrior. Having Hard without Soft leads to a lack of adaptability and flexibility in specific situations. Conversely, having Soft without Hard fails to achieve maximum effectiveness."

• Applying Scientific Principles

Like other martial arts, Vovinam applies scientific principles to martial arts such as: centrifugal force (spinning moves, deflections, parries, punches, kicks, elbow strikes... following an arc or circle); lever force (locks, joint breaks, hooks, blocks...), rotational force (straight punches...), flexion force and explosive power (throws, slams, grappling, jumps...), etc., all intended to help practitioners use less energy while executing techniques yet still achieving high effectiveness.
In particular, techniques such as sweeping chops, disabling chops, reverse-direction elbow strikes (hand and foot forces delivered simultaneously but in opposite directions), takedown strikes (hand and leg forces delivered simultaneously and in the same direction) along with neck-grappling moves (deployed suddenly when an opponent is careless, off-balance...) within the basic legwork system used to take down opponents are also important technical hallmarks of Vovinam.

• The Principle of “One Becomes Three”

Another notable point is that solo forms (unarmed forms, weapon forms), paired drills (2 practitioners performing a sequence of unarmed or armed techniques according to convention), and group drills (3-4 practitioners performing a sequence of unarmed or armed techniques according to convention) are essentially logical connections of escapes, basic counter-techniques... to create favorable conditions for students to review and practice. This is exactly the principle of "one develops into three" in the technical system of the discipline...
Over the past decade, Vovinam has added several Soft Energy Forms (Nhu Khí Công Quyền) for all practitioners and Continuous Paired Forms for older practitioners, consisting of gentle movements without falls. Constantly supplemented over the past 40 years, Vovinam’s system of techniques, unarmed forms, and weapons (knife, sword, staff, hammer, machete, ruler, saber, great saber...) ensures the original basic characteristics while bearing both Vietnamese tradition and modernity.
